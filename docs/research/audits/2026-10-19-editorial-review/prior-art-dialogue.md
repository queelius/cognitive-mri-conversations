# Prior-art audit: dialogue systems, NLP, sentence embeddings, human-LLM logs, research practice

Audit date: 2026-10-19. Manuscript audited: `papers/iscs-2027/paper/main.tex` (ISCS 2027
long paper, "What the Whole Conversation Predicts"), Sect. 2 and the Introduction, against
`references.bib` and the earlier audit `papers/history-falsification/audits/literature.md`
(2026-10-17). Network-science prior art (graph growth, temporal networks, densification) is
covered by a separate audit and is not treated here.

Verification rule applied: every paper listed in Sect. 2 of this file was opened on a primary
page (arXiv abstract or full text, ACL Anthology, PMLR, Crossref record, or the publisher PDF)
during this audit. Where a venue could not be confirmed from a primary page the entry says so.
Papers already in `references.bib` (CCL, Triple-Encoders, OnePred, Flipping the Dialogue,
Ohagi et al., Chi et al., Ghosal et al., ChatbotID) were not re-opened; their characterizations
in the earlier audit were re-read against the manuscript text and found consistent.

Companion note: `pooling-diagnostics.md` in this directory (written by the parallel review on
development embeddings) reports that pooling j unrelated donor vectors against 2 unrelated
donor vectors already yields +.020 to +.033 raw cosine, about zero under centering, and that
FIRST+WIN2 matches or beats MEAN. Those numbers are cited below where they bear on novelty;
they are development-data diagnostics, not a change to the frozen result.

### A. History versus recency in next-turn prediction (dialogue systems, multi-turn LLM evaluation)

- **A-3. Wu, Wu, Xing, Li, Zhou (2017). - **A-4. Laban, Hayashi, Zhou, Neville (2026). LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation.** ICLR 2026 (header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026" read from the proceedings PDF at proceedings.iclr.cc); arXiv:2505.06120. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120 . - **A-5. Huang, Choshen, Sun, Khattab, Fernandez Astudillo, Damani, Broderick, Andreas (2026). - **A-6. Naous, Laban, Xu, Neville (2026). Flipping the Dialogue: Training and Evaluating User Language Models.** ICLR 2026 (arXiv:2510.06552v2 comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06552 . Already cited; L43 describes only its data, not its finding. [C1 framing]
- **A-7. Zhou, Zhang, Xiao, Dingwall, Ma, Arnold, Xiang (2022). Learning Dialogue Representations from Consecutive Utterances.** NAACL 2022, pp. 754-768. ### C. Embedding geometry: why pooling more vectors raises raw cosine, and what control to use

- **C-1. Mitra, Kumar (2026). Mean-Pooled Cosine Similarity is Not Length-Invariant: Theory and Cross-Domain Evidence for a Length-Invariant Alternative.** arXiv:2605.07345 (May 2026; submitted to the ICML 2026 Mechanistic Interpretability workshop; not peer reviewed). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07345 . Model: h_i = mu + sigma eps_i with a shared direction mu; the mean of n items keeps mu and shrinks the noise by 1/sqrt(n). Proposition 1: for two independent sets of m and n i.i.d. vectors with common mean mu and covariance sigma^2 I, E[cos(xbar, ybar)] is approximately 1 / [sqrt(1 + rho/m) sqrt(1 + rho/n)] with rho = sigma^2 d / ||mu||^2, "strictly increasing in both m and n"; first order, E[cos] is about 1 - (rho/2)(1/m + 1/n). The full text states the derivation needs only i.i.d. vectors with a common mean, not tokens of one sequence, so it applies to pooled message vectors. Recommends CKA; length alone explains 52 to 75 percent of cross-language cosine in code LLMs. **This is the a priori prediction of C1's sign and 1/n saturation.** With m = 1 (a single target) the predicted gain of n over 2 is f(n) - f(2), f(n) = (1 + rho)^(-1/2) (1 + rho/n)^(-1/2). Auditor's back-of-envelope, to be redone by the authors: fitting rho to the MiniLM last-two level of .38 gives rho of about 2.2 and a predicted gain at n = 10 of about +.12, which matches process A's +.120 and is about five times the empirical +.025; for E5 (level .85, rho about .22) the prediction is about +.045 against +.018 observed. [C1, C3, C4] [2025-2026]
- **C-2. Zhou, Dai, Cao, Zhang, Xu (2025). Length-Induced Embedding Collapse in PLM-based Models.** ACL 2025 (arXiv:2410.24200v2 comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24200 . Embeddings of longer texts cluster together ("length collapse"), attributed to self-attention acting as a low-pass filter; TempScale narrows the short/long gap (+0.94 MTEB, +1.10 LongEmbed). A related length artifact that bears on the 240-token and chunked views. [C1, C5] [2025-2026]
- **C-3. Gao, Xu, Mei, Metaxas (2026). Pooling and Semantic Shift: The Fundamental Challenges in Long Text Embedding and Retrieval.** arXiv:2603.21437 (v2, May 2026; no venue). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21437 . Argues that contextual pooling intrinsically causes embedding collapse: pooling semantically diverse sentences dilutes meaning and reduces mean pairwise distance; semantic shift, not length, predicts concentration. Bears on averaging over a drifting conversation and on whole-versus-parts encoding. [C1, C5] [2025-2026]
- **C-4. Ethayarajh (2019). How Contextual are Contextualized Word Representations? Comparing the Geometry of BERT, ELMo, and GPT-2 Embeddings.** EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019, pp. 55-65. https://aclanthology.org/D19-1006/ . Representations are anisotropic in every layer of ELMo, BERT and GPT-2. The standard citation for a positive cosine floor between unrelated texts; the manuscript reports an E5 baseline level of .85 without it. [C1; missing]
- **C-5. Mu, Bhat, Viswanath (2018). All-but-the-Top: Simple and Effective Postprocessing for Word Representations.** arXiv:1702.01417v2 (opened); ICLR 2018 venue not verified from a primary page because OpenReview and dblp blocked automated access during this audit. https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01417 . Removing the common mean vector and a few top directions improves word and sentence-level similarity tasks. The centering remedy that the manuscript's centered-cosine secondary implements without citation. [C1]
- **C-6. Timkey, van Schijndel (2021). All Bark and No Bite: Rogue Dimensions in Transformer Language Models Obscure Representational Quality.** EMNLP 2021, pp. 4527-4546. DOI 10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.372. https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.372/ . One to three rogue dimensions dominate cosine in contextual models and are not the ones the model relies on; standardization removes the distortion. Recommends standardization, stronger than mean removal. [C1]
- **C-7. Steck, Ekanadham, Kallus (2024). Is Cosine-Similarity of Embeddings Really About Similarity?** WWW 2024 Companion; arXiv:2403.05440. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05440 . In regularized linear models cosine between learned embeddings can be arbitrary or dictated by the regularizer; cautions against blind use and outlines alternatives. [C4]
- **C-8. Parupudi (2026). Anisotropy Decides Cosine vs. Rank Metrics for Text Embeddings.** arXiv:2606.29571 (Jun 2026; no venue). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29571 . 19 parameter-free metrics, 19 encoders, 7 datasets: when variance concentrates in a few directions, rank-based and L1-type metrics beat cosine by about 20 percent relative; the variance share of the single most dominant dimension predicts the gain (rank correlation 0.86); projecting out dominant directions restores cosine. Supplies a one-number diagnostic the authors could report for MiniLM and E5 and a reason cosine and MRR need not agree. [C4] [2025-2026]

### E. Chunking, re-encoding, compositionality

- **E-1. Günther, Mohr, Williams, Wang, Xiao (2024). Late Chunking: Contextual Chunk Embeddings Using Long-Context Embedding Models.** arXiv:2409.04701v3 (Jul 2025; no venue). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04701 . Naive chunking encodes chunks independently and loses surrounding context; chunking after the transformer and before mean pooling yields chunk embeddings that carry full context and retrieve better without training. The premise of C5 (re-encoded halves differ from the whole) is this paper's starting point. [C5]
- **E-2. Bhat, Rudat, Spiekermann, Flores-Herr (2025). Rethinking Chunk Size For Long-Document Retrieval: A Multi-Dataset Analysis.** arXiv:2505.21700 (no venue). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21700 . Chunks of 64 to 1024 tokens, six datasets, two embedding models; the best size depends on dataset and model (Stella prefers larger, Snowflake smaller). Chunk-size sensitivity is documented. [C5] [2025-2026]
- **E-3. Shaukat, Adnan, Kuhn (2026). A Systematic Investigation of Document Chunking Strategies and Embedding Sensitivity.** arXiv:2603.06976 (no venue). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06976 . 36 chunking methods, six domains, five embedding models; paragraph grouping reaches nDCG@5 of about 0.459 while fixed-size character chunking stays below 0.244; larger models remain sensitive to poor segmentation. [C5] [2025-2026]
- **E-4. Guo, Xue, Xu, Bo, Ye, Pierrehumbert, Lewis (2025). Quantifying Compositionality of Classic and State-of-the-Art Embeddings.** Findings of EMNLP 2025; arXiv:2509.19332. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19332 . 2. **L38**: "the next thing a person says to an assistant is predicted better, in embedding space, by the whole prior exchange than by the latest exchange." Supported only by two trained-ranking papers; the standard dialogue citations are Tian et al. 2017, Wu et al. 2017 and Sankar et al. 2019, and 2026 in-the-wild evidence points the other way for use (Huang et al. 2026). ## 4. The three most damaging things a knowledgeable NLP reviewer could say

1. "Your primary result is a proposition, not a finding. For any set of vectors sharing a common direction, the expected cosine between their mean and a target increases monotonically in the number averaged and saturates like 1/n (Mitra and Kumar 2026, Prop. 1; anisotropy per Ethayarajh 2019 and Timkey and van Schijndel 2021). Your budget curve is that curve. Your own process A, a fixed topic with no older-state dependence, reproduces the gain at +.120, five times the empirical value, and pooling unrelated donor vectors gives the headline +.02 to +.03 that vanishes under centering. Until the pre-registered contrast is reported against that null, 'what the whole conversation predicts' is a statement about the mean vector."

2. "The pre-registration confirms a sign that was never in doubt. Full history beating the latest exchange is reported for trained rankers (Erker et al. 2023) and prompted LLMs (Chen et al. 2026); context-length saturation near ten utterances is in Wu et al. 2017; and for the quantity that matters, response quality, the most recent exchange often matches full context on real logs (Huang et al. 2026). Replicating a geometry-predicted .02 cosine difference on two corpora, with a negative ranking result, is a replication note. The honest contribution is the observation-process lesson, and that lesson does not need twelve pages."

3. "Nothing here is specific to human-AI dialogue or to a complex system. The encoders are frozen 384-dimensional sentence models; the result would hold for any sequence of related texts. The role asymmetry reduces to information-set bookkeeping that dialogue work has handled since Chi et al. 2017, the packaging result restates the premise of late chunking (Günther et al. 2024), and the user-simulation and next-utterance-prediction literatures that own this task (Naous et al. 2026; Wang et al. 2025; Kruff et al. 2025) are not engaged. What property of the system did we learn?"
