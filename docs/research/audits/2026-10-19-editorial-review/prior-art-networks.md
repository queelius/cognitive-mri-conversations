# Prior-art audit: network science, sampling and observation nulls (ISCS 2027 long paper and NetSci 2027 abstract)

Search and source-inspection date: 2026-10-19. Scope: network science, temporal
networks, graph growth and sampling, measurement artifacts, semantic and document
similarity networks. Dialogue-NLP and embedding geometry were covered by a second
auditor and are excluded here. Files audited (read-only): `papers/iscs-2027/paper/main.tex`
(line numbers below refer to that file), `papers/iscs-2027/paper/references.bib`,
`papers/iscs-2027/abstracts/netsci-2027/main.tex`, and the earlier audit
`papers/history-falsification/audits/literature.md` (its twelve items are taken as known
and are not repeated).

Every entry below was opened at a primary or publisher record (full text, publisher
abstract, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar or Crossref record). Items with "record only" were
opened only as a bibliographic record and carry no content claim beyond the title.
Items marked **2025-2026** may be unknown to the authors.

## 2. Annotated bibliography (25 verified entries, grouped by question)

### Q1. Densification from sampling or incomplete observation of a fixed graph

1. **Pedarsani, Figueiredo and Grossglauser, "Densification arising from sampling fixed graphs", ACM SIGMETRICS 2008, pp. 205-216.** DOI 10.1145/1375457.1375481; full text opened at https://indy.epfl.ch/grossglauser/Papers/sigmx08_dens.pdf. A fixed underlying graph G=(V,E) is revealed by including each edge independently with probability p_e and taking the endpoints as discovered nodes; E[e(p)] = n_e p; densification follows "based on how we observe the fixed underlying graph, without requiring any dynamic growth process"; with a Zipf degree distribution of exponent s the asymptotic densification exponent is alpha = 1/(s-1) for s < 2 and 1 otherwise; applied to an 89-week EPFL email log the model gives alpha-hat = 1.8 against 1.57 observed, and weekly (non-accumulated) snapshots give 1.89. Section 3.3 "Observation Models" already distinguishes accumulation from fixed-window observation. This is the direct antecedent of C6; it is absent from references.bib.
2. **Leskovec and Faloutsos, "Sampling from large graphs", KDD 2006.** Full text opened at https://cs.stanford.edu/people/jure/pubs/sampling-kdd06.pdf. Defines the "Back-in-time" sampling goal (match the graph as it grew) and tests Random Node sampling, i.e. the induced subgraph on uniformly chosen vertices; on the HEP-PH citation graph "FF, RN and RPN match the temporal densification of the true graph" (Sect. 4.3.1, Fig. 2a). This is the manuscript's revelation null used as a sampling method and found to reproduce the empirical densification power law.
3. **Leskovec, Kleinberg and Faloutsos, "Graph evolution: densification and shrinking diameters", ACM TKDD 1(1), 2007.** arXiv physics/0603229, HTML opened at https://arxiv.org/html/physics/0603229v2. Defines e(t) proportional to n(t)^a with a between 1 and 2 (arXiv 1.68, HEP-PH 1.56, patents 1.66, AS 1.18, affiliation 1.08-1.15, email 1.12); names the "missing past" and "phantom nodes" artifact of the observation window and judges it "relatively minor once we move away from the beginning of the observation period", with the rigorous reset test applied to diameter rather than densification. The densification law the manuscript's null targets; not cited in the manuscript.
4. **Stumpf, Wiuf and May, "Subnets of scale-free networks are not scale-free: sampling properties of networks", PNAS 102(12):4221-4224, 2005.** DOI 10.1073/pnas.0501179102 (Europe PMC record opened). Random node sampling of a fixed network preserves the degree-distribution family for classical and exponential random graphs but not for scale-free ones. The standard warning that induced-subgraph observation changes the statistics one reads off a fixed graph.
5. **Lee, Kim and Jeong, "Statistical properties of sampled networks", Phys. Rev. E 73, 016102, 2006.** arXiv cond-mat/0505232. Three sampling methods (node, link, snowball) give different and systematically biased degree exponents, path lengths, betweenness, assortativity and clustering; the bias directions are tabulated (reproduced in Kolaczyk's 2014 slides). Prior that "the sampling procedure biases these estimates" is established and quantified.
6. **Kossinets, "Effects of missing data in social networks", arXiv cond-mat/0306335, 2003** (journal version not opened). Boundary specification, non-response and fixed-choice censoring each bias clustering, assortativity and network-level statistics in known directions. Early catalogue of observation-process artifacts in social networks.
7. **Kolaczyk, "Sampling and estimation in network graphs", ch. 5 of Statistical Analysis of Network Data, Springer 2009** (Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-88146-1_5) **and Kolaczyk, "Network sampling" slides, Workshop on Private Analysis of Social Networks, 19 May 2014,** opened at https://www.bu.edu/cs/files/2014/05/Kolaczyk.pdf. Induced subgraph sampling is the first named design; "completely ignoring sampling issues is equivalent to using plug-in estimators"; estimation under these designs "goes back to the 1970's ... Frank and colleagues, based mainly on Horvitz-Thompson theory". Under simple random sampling of k of N vertices without replacement the pair-inclusion probability is k(k-1)/(N(N-1)), which is the manuscript's identity. (Frank, "Sampling and estimation in large social networks", Social Networks 1:91-101, 1978, DOI 10.1016/0378-8733(78)90015-1, record only.)
8. **Krings, Karsai, Bernhardsson, Blondel and Saramaki, "Effects of time window size and placement on the structure of an aggregated communication network", EPJ Data Science 1:4, 2012.** arXiv 1202.1145, DOI 10.1140/epjds4. In mobile-phone data the aggregated network's "average degree keeps growing even for intervals up to 180 days" and placement over weekends or holidays changes structure. A pure observation-window effect that produces growth curves; relevant to "when each feature became available".
9. **Jeong, Neda and Barabasi, "Measuring preferential attachment for evolving networks", Europhys. Lett. 61:567-572, 2003.** arXiv cond-mat/0104131. Attachment is measured from the time order in which nodes and links appear; a vertex-order shuffle of the kind the manuscript proposes is also a null for this measurement, which the manuscript could say when it claims the curve "needs no attachment".

### Q2. Denominator and activity-definition sensitivity of growth exponents

10. **Kobayashi and Genois, "Two types of densification scaling in the evolution of temporal networks", Phys. Rev. E 102, 052302, 2020.** arXiv 2005.09445, full text opened. "We define N to be the number of nodes that have at least one edge in a given snapshot"; the potential population N_p (active plus inactive) "is not" observable; a rise in activity "would cause the number of active nodes N to rise ... This increases the denominator of the density by definition". The densification power law M proportional to N^gamma with 1 < gamma < 2 is attributed to Leskovec et al. The active-node convention of the manuscript's A_k is this N; the manuscript cites the paper (line 47) but as "densification denominators" without saying this.
11. **Kobayashi and Genois, "The switching mechanisms of social network densification", Scientific Reports 11:3160, 2021.** DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-82432-9, PMC7862646 opened. Bayesian regime switching between population-driven and activity-driven densification; N = nodes with at least one edge, N_p "the total of active and inactive nodes that are in the room"; warns that (N, M) pairs alone may not recover N_p and kappa in small networks. Cite with 10 for the active-versus-potential denominator.
12. **Arcaute, Hatna, Ferguson, Youn, Johansson and Batty, "Constructing cities, deconstructing scaling laws", J. R. Soc. Interface 2015.** DOI 10.1098/rsif.2014.0745, arXiv 1301.1674. Thousands of alternative city definitions for England and Wales; where non-linear scaling exists "the exponent fluctuates considerably" with the unit definition. The closest general precedent for "the denominator alone moves the fitted exponent".
13. **Leitao, Miotto, Gerlach and Altmann, "Is this scaling nonlinear?", R. Soc. Open Sci. 3:150649, 2016.** arXiv 1604.02872. Whether beta differs from 1 "crucially depend[s] on the fluctuations contained in the data" and how they are modelled; five models on fifteen datasets. Supports the manuscript's caveat that its identities are expectations, "not the expected value of a fitted slope", and shows the fitted-slope question has its own literature.

(Also opened: Ferguson and Kobayashi, "Identifying the temporal dynamics of densification and sparsification in human contact networks", EPJ Data Science 11:52, 2022, arXiv 2201.09489, a maximum-likelihood estimator of population and connection probability from aggregate (N, M) only; Dorogovtsev and Mendes, "Effect of the accelerating growth of communications networks on their structure", Phys. Rev. E 63, 025101, 2001, arXiv cond-mat/0009065, the growth-mechanism account of edges outpacing nodes.)

### Q3. Temporal-network null models, "what each control preserves", and reporting requirements

14. **Gauvin, Genois, Karsai, Kivela, Takaguchi, Valdano and Vestergaard, "Randomized reference models for temporal networks", SIAM Review 64(4):763-830, 2022.** arXiv 1806.04032, full text opened. Motivation: "there are no unified naming conventions for the RRMs" and shuffling effects "remain poorly understood, rendering their use non-trivial and susceptible to misinterpretation"; Definition II.10: a model constraining features x_1,...,x_Q "is named P[x_1, x_2, ..., x_Q]"; the framework is for "instant event temporal networks with a fixed set of nodes and observation interval". The manuscript's "state what each control preserves" is this convention; the manuscript's vertex-order null is outside the fixed-node-set scope and should be named rather than cited to it (line 155).
15. **Holme and Saramaki, "Temporal networks", Physics Reports 519:97-125, 2012.** arXiv 1108.1780, full text opened. Its section on randomized reference models lists (randomized edges, randomly permuted times, random times, random contacts, equal-weight edge randomization, edge randomization) and what each keeps; on observation: "For long enough periods of observation, another difficulty is posed by the dynamics of vertices entering and exiting the system. If only edge activation sequences are observed, such vertex dynamics cannot generally be distinguished from edge dynamics", with the remedy of including only vertices known to be present for the whole period. This is the "population denominator" item of C7, stated in 2012.
16. **Fosdick, Larremore, Nishimura and Ugander, "Configuring random graph models with fixed degree sequences", SIAM Review 2018.** arXiv 1608.00607. A configuration-model null must specify its graph space (stub- or vertex-labelled; loops; multiedges); the three vignettes show "substantial differences in study conclusions under different models". Precedent that the specification of what a control preserves changes conclusions, not only interpretation.
17. **Vasa and Misic, "Null models in network neuroscience", Nature Reviews Neuroscience 23:493-504, 2022.** DOI 10.1038/s41583-022-00601-9 (Europe PMC record). Taxonomy of randomization and generative nulls ordered from liberal to conservative, including nulls for correlation-derived (thresholded similarity) networks. The nearest existing field-level guidance for reporting which features a null preserves; the manuscript's networks are correlation-derived in this sense.
18. **Olteanu, Castillo, Diaz and Kiciman, "Social data: biases, methodological pitfalls, and ethical boundaries", Frontiers in Big Data 2:13, 2019.** DOI 10.3389/fdata.2019.00013, full text opened. Catalogue of population, behavioural, temporal ("time granularity that is too fine or too coarse"), acquisition and sampling biases in platform data, with opaque sampling (e.g. 1 percent API streams) and platform design as sources. The existing checklist for "when each feature became available" and "population" on data such as WildChat and PRISM.

(Also opened: Wagner, Strohmaier, Olteanu, Kiciman, Contractor and Eliassi-Rad, "Measuring algorithmically infused societies", Nature 595:197-204, 2021, DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-03666-1, which calls for auditable measurement models for platform data; Gebru et al., "Datasheets for datasets", Commun. ACM Dec. 2021, arXiv 1803.09010, whose template documents the collection process; Ribeiro, Perra and Baronchelli, "Quantifying the effect of temporal resolution on time-varying networks", Sci. Rep. 3:3006, 2013, arXiv 1211.7052, on the bias introduced by the aggregation window; Gotelli, "Null model analysis of species co-occurrence patterns", Ecology 81:2606-2621, 2000, record only, the ecological precedent for classifying nulls by which margins they fix.)

### Q4. Semantic similarity networks from documents and conversations; the "cognitive" framing; threshold artifacts

19. **Siew, Wulff, Beckage and Kenett, "Cognitive network science: a review of research on cognition through the lens of network representations, processes, and dynamics", Complexity 2019:2108423.** DOI 10.1155/2019/2108423 (Semantic Scholar record). Defines the field: networks represent cognitive systems (semantic and lexical), structure-process interaction explains behaviour, and network change over timescales is a core question. The term "cognitive" in network science is taken; the manuscript's networks are over documents, not concepts.
20. **Haim and Stella, "Cognitive networks for knowledge modeling: a gentle introduction for data- and cognitive scientists", WIREs Cognitive Science 17(2):e70026, 2026. 2025-2026.** DOI 10.1002/wcs.70026 (Crossref record). "Cognitive networks are representations of associative knowledge between concepts"; links are semantic, syntactic, phonological or visual relations. Current statement of the definition the "cognitive MRI" framing collides with.
21. **De Duro, Improta and Stella, "Introducing CounseLLMe: a dataset of simulated mental health dialogues for comparing LLMs like Haiku, LLaMAntino and ChatGPT against humans", Emerging Trends in Drugs, Addictions, and Health 5:100170, 2025. 2025-2026.** DOI 10.1016/j.etdah.2025.100170 (IRIS Trento record). Textual forma mentis networks (concept nodes, syntactic and semantic links) built from 400 LLM-LLM dialogues and compared with human conversations. Precedent for network analysis of LLM conversation text, using concept networks rather than document-similarity thresholds. (Abramski, Citraro, Lombardi, Rossetti and Stella, "Cognitive network science reveals bias in GPT-3, ChatGPT, and GPT-4 ...", arXiv 2305.18320, 2023, also opened: behavioural forma mentis networks from LLM free-association prompts.)
22. **Chavalarias and Cointet, "Phylomemetic patterns in science evolution: the rise and fall of scientific fields", PLoS ONE 8(2):e54847, 2013.** Full text opened. Yearly term co-occurrence networks from 200,000 abstracts, thresholded to directed unweighted graphs, fields as cliques tracked over time; field density is read as viability, and the authors note the threshold choice is "somewhat arbitrary". A thresholded semantic network whose temporal density is interpreted as development; the practice the manuscript's Sect. 5 criticises, and currently uncited at line 149. (Bettencourt, Kaiser and Kaur, "Scientific discovery and topological transitions in collaboration networks", J. Informetrics 3:210-221, 2009, DOI 10.1016/j.joi.2009.03.001, Semantic Scholar abstract opened: densification and giant-component transitions read as signatures of discovery.)
23. **van Wijk, Stam and Daffertshofer, "Comparing brain networks of different size and connectivity density using graph theory", PLoS ONE 5(10):e13701, 2010.** Full text opened. Graph measures depend on node count and density; fixing them by threshold, normalising by random surrogates and estimating size effects each carry bias; "none of the here-investigated methods allows for a reliable and fully unbiased comparison". The standard reference that thresholded-similarity network statistics are not comparable across sizes and densities, which bears directly on reading edge counts of a growing thresholded network as development.
24. **Zahoranszky-Kohalmi, Bologa and Oprea, "Impact of similarity threshold on the topology of molecular similarity networks and clustering outcomes", J. Cheminformatics 8, 2016.** DOI 10.1186/s13321-016-0127-5 (Europe PMC record). Clustering coefficient against threshold shows a peak following a steep drop in edge count; the peak aligns with the best clustering outcome. Shows that community results in cosine-threshold networks are threshold artifacts unless the threshold is chosen against a reference. (Also opened: Molnar and Csala, "Threshold sensitivity of the production network topology", Applied Network Science 8:71, 2023, DOI 10.1007/s41109-023-00599-8: "altering even minor threshold values significantly reshapes the network's structure"; Hayasaka, "Explosive percolation in thresholded networks", Physica A 451:1-9, 2016, arXiv 1504.00050: similarity-thresholded networks can show abrupt giant-component emergence.)

### Q5. Chunk-size and segmentation sensitivity of embedding or text networks

25. **Akimushkin, Amancio and Oliveira, "Text authorship identified using the dynamics of word co-occurrence networks", PLoS ONE 12(1):e0170527, 2017.** Full text opened. Texts are cut into consecutive 200-token pieces chosen by balancing series length against network stability, because "some network measurements are sensitive to network size" and varying piece size would "generate a noise". Direct precedent that the text unit is a free parameter of text networks that must be fixed and reported.

(Also opened: Shaukat, Adnan and Kuhn, "A systematic investigation of document chunking strategies and embedding sensitivity", arXiv 2603.06976, March 2026, **2025-2026**: 36 chunking strategies, five encoders, six domains; content-aware chunking beats fixed-length splitting and "larger embedding models yield higher absolute scores but remain sensitive to suboptimal segmentation". Chen, Rockmore, Latifi Jebelli, Riddell and Stropkay, "On the literary landscapes of vector embeddings", Computational Humanities Research 1:e18, 2025, DOI 10.1017/chr.2025.10015, **2025-2026**: 1,024-token chunks, with 512-token encoders run on two halves and averaged, i.e. the manuscript's "subdivide" recipe; the authors acknowledge chunk length may change results and run no sensitivity analysis. Tamkin et al., "Clio: privacy-preserving insights into real-world AI use", arXiv 2412.13678, Dec. 2024: hierarchical cluster summaries of conversations, no network. Rosen, Miagkikh and Suthers, "Social and semantic network analysis of chat logs", LAK 2011, DOI 10.1145/2090116.2090137, record only. Carley and Palmquist, "Extracting, representing, and analyzing mental models", Social Forces 70:601, 1992, DOI 10.2307/2579746, record only: the lineage of inferring cognitive structure from text networks. Paranyushkin, "InfraNodus: generating insight using text network analysis", WWW 2019 Companion, pp. 3584-3589, DOI 10.1145/3308558.3314123, record only.)

## 3. Sentences in the manuscript that are inaccurate, unsupported, or missing an obvious citation

Line numbers are those of `papers/iscs-2027/paper/main.tex`; "NetSci L48" is line 48 of the abstract.

1. **L36:** "Archives of conversations with AI assistants are now a common object of complex-systems study." Unsupported: the only citations are the authors' own two papers. The corpora cited later (WildChat, PRISM, Clio) are NLP and alignment work, not complex-systems studies. Either cite independent complex-systems work on such archives or soften to "have begun to be studied".
2. **L40:** "two exact results showing that message packaging and the order in which a fixed graph is revealed manufacture apparent dynamics on their own." Novelty wording is inaccurate for the revelation result: the thesis is Pedarsani et al. 2008 (entry 1), the empirical observation is Leskovec and Faloutsos 2006 (entry 2), and the identity is simple-random-sampling pair inclusion (entry 7). Say "two elementary identities, the second a special case of induced-subgraph sampling theory".
3. **L47:** "The antecedents of Sect. 5 are densification denominators \cite{kobayashi2020densification} and randomized reference models for temporal networks \cite{gauvin2022randomized}." Missing the obvious antecedents: Leskovec, Kleinberg and Faloutsos (2005, 2007), who defined the densification law and the "missing past" artifact; Pedarsani et al. 2008; and the sampling-theory line (Frank 1978; Stumpf 2005; Lee 2006; Kolaczyk 2009). "Densification denominators" also under-describes Kobayashi and Genois, whose point is the active-versus-potential node count (entry 10).
4. **L147:** "Pooling recipes are often written as decaying weights over a token clock" and the chunk-sensitivity numbers carry no citation to the segmentation-sensitivity literature (entries 25 and the Shaukat and Chen items). "Often" is unsupported as written.
5. **L149:** "Conversation networks are often grown in time order and their edge count read as development." No citation. The practice is documented for term and collaboration networks (Chavalarias and Cointet 2013; Bettencourt et al. 2009) and the authors' own rejected temporal paper; cite those or write "we did this in our temporal analysis".
6. **L149-152:** "defined by whole-conversation embeddings and a similarity threshold" followed by an identity that holds for any graph: say so, and cite the threshold-artifact literature (entries 23, 24) for why the endpoint graph itself is not a fixed object.
7. **L155:** "Both identities hold for every endpoint graph (checked by exhaustive enumeration on small graphs)." Reads as an empirical check of a theorem. E[M_k] follows in one line from exchangeability of vertex order (each pair has inclusion probability k(k-1)/(N(N-1))); E[A_k] likewise. Replace with the derivation and cite entry 7. Keep the enumeration only as a code test in the repository.
8. **L155:** "A superlinear edge curve therefore needs no clustering, attachment or cognition". True, but this is Pedarsani et al.'s conclusion ("without requiring any dynamic growth process that modifies its structure") and the manuscript should say how its null differs: vertex revelation gives asymptotic exponent 2 for every graph, edge sampling gives alpha = 1/(s-1) only for s < 2. Also "attachment" should cite Jeong et al. 2003, since the vertex-order shuffle is a null for the attachment-rate measurement as well.
9. **L155:** "the choice of k or A_k as denominator changes a fitted densification exponent materially (1.56 against 1.41 on our author archive)." Ambiguous: which denominator gives which. Missing: the null's own fitted exponent under each denominator, without which the reader cannot tell whether the archive densifies more or less than random revelation. Under the k denominator the null expectation is quadratic, so the null exponent is 2 and both empirical values lie below it.
10. **L155:** "a residual after subtracting them is conditional on the exchangeability the null assumes \cite{gauvin2022randomized}." Gauvin et al. define their models on "a fixed set of nodes and observation interval"; vertex-order randomization is not among their MRRMs. Cite Gauvin for the convention and name the revelation null in that convention as a vertex-order shuffle that preserves the endpoint graph, or cite entry 2, where the same null appears as Random Node sampling under the back-in-time goal.
11. **L168:** "What the five cases share is a reporting requirement: state the unit retained, when each feature became available, the population denominator, the permitted prefix, and what each control preserves." Each item has a named precedent (entries 14, 15, 7, 18); present it as assembled from them. As written it reads as if new.
12. **L176:** "The twelve-message, 128-token cap retains over 99% of user tokens but about three quarters of assistant tokens on PRISM" is fine, but the implication for the revelation null (vertices "defined by whole-conversation embeddings") is that the endpoint graph used at L149 was built under a different visibility than the rest of the paper; say which embedding and threshold defined G.
13. **NetSci L48:** "The same hazard has an exact form for conversation networks ... so a superlinear edge curve needs no attachment, clustering or cognition, and the choice of denominator alone moves a fitted densification exponent from 1.41 to 1.56". Same three problems as items 8 and 9 (no Pedarsani or Leskovec citation; which denominator; no null exponent). The reference list [1]-[4] has no densification or sampling reference at all; a NetSci reviewer will expect Leskovec et al. 2005/2007 and Pedarsani et al. 2008 by name.

## 4. The three most damaging things a network-science reviewer could say about the observation-nulls contribution

1. "Section 5's revelation null is Pedarsani, Figueiredo and Grossglauser (SIGMETRICS 2008) with vertices sampled instead of edges, and Leskovec and Faloutsos (KDD 2006) already showed that random-node samples of the final graph reproduce the densification power law of the real growth. The identity E[M_k] = M k(k-1)/(N(N-1)) is the pair-inclusion probability of simple random sampling without replacement, the first example in Kolaczyk's sampling chapter and in Frank's 1978 Horvitz-Thompson treatment. The paper cites none of these, cites Leskovec et al. nowhere, and calls the result exact and new. This is a missing-related-work problem of the kind that ends a review."

2. "The null's own exponent is never reported. Under uniform revelation with the all-nodes denominator the expectation is quadratic in k, so the null exponent is 2, and both empirical values (1.41 and 1.56) lie below it; under the active-node denominator no null value is given at all. 'The choice of denominator alone moves the exponent from 1.41 to 1.56' is therefore uninterpretable: the reader cannot tell whether the archive densifies more or less than random order, and an archive that is sub-null under one convention and unreported under the other is not a demonstration that the null matters, only that two conventions exist, which Leskovec et al. and Kobayashi and Genois already embody."

3. "The 'reporting requirement' is a list of things the field already requires. Gauvin et al.'s naming convention exists precisely so that one states what each reference model preserves; Holme and Saramaki (2012) already warn that vertex entry and exit cannot be separated from edge dynamics when only edge activations are observed; Kolaczyk's sampling-design chapter is 'the unit retained'; Olteanu et al. catalogue temporal and population biases of platform data. Packaging these for conversation archives is not a contribution unless a published conclusion is shown to change under it, and the only example offered is the authors' own exponent from a rejected paper, computed on a network this paper does not otherwise analyse. Section 5 is a comment on the authors' prior work attached to an unrelated prediction study."

A fourth, shorter one the editors should expect: "If the endpoint graph is defined by a cosine threshold, its edge count is itself a threshold artifact (van Wijk et al. 2010; Zahoranszky-Kohalmi et al. 2016), so the question of what a growing edge count means has to be answered before any arrival-order null is relevant."
