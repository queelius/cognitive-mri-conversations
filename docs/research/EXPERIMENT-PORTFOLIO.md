## Six connected questions and twelve proposed experiments

The identifiers below identify distinct questions or comparisons. Changing a seed, threshold or encoder is usually a sensitivity analysis within an experiment, not a new independent confirmation.

### Q1. When does spanning topics become integration?

- **P01 — Validate the distinction.** On a development sample of public exchanges, independently code explicit relations/applications, sequential topic switches, topic lists and ambiguous cases. Sample ordinary and low-scoring exchanges as well as apparent bridges. Estimate coding agreement and whether enough cases exist. With a representative evaluation sample, estimate prevalence and variation by task; a bridge-enriched convenience sample cannot supply population prevalence.
- **P02 — Test what the network adds.** Compare conversation-level bridges, episode-level concept relations and hierarchical measures against direct text, topic composition, length, frequency and role mixture for the independently coded outcome. Fit/choose on development data and evaluate at the conversation or participant grouping level.

**Builds on:** static bridge taxonomy, hierarchy paper and semantic episodes. **Evidence against the proposed interpretation:** bridges mostly identify switching or lists, or add no practically useful information with sufficiently precise estimates. Unreliable labels instead limit what can be concluded. The empirical prevalence and process question can remain interesting even if the graph adds little.

### Q2. How do user and assistant contributions develop together?

- **P03 — Test incremental cross-role history.** For a future observable fixed before analysis, compare models using task/current content and own-role history with models also using the partner's earlier contributions. Use both prediction directions and comparable model capacity. Strong baselines include the immediately preceding message and ordinary autoregression/state-space models. Evaluate only genuinely prior information.
- **P04 — Relate interaction to development.** Test whether independently coded challenge, revision and reformulation sequences distinguish integration from echoing or topic continuity. Compare these event sequences with role-trajectory alignment and matched task/length controls. Check whether apparent alignment persists in synthetic independent streams driven by the same topic and in carefully matched surrogate exchanges.

**Builds on:** Semantic Dynamics role trajectories and the user's observation that connections emerge through back-and-forth. **Evidence against a stronger coupling claim:** the apparent gain disappears after immediate-response and shared-topic controls, or is reproduced by uncoupled controls. Predictability does not identify causal influence, intellectual credit or learning. A shuffled reply's incoherence can make a control artificially easy.

### Q3. What persists, and on which timescale?

- **P05 — Estimate useful history.** Compare last-message, fixed-window, uniform-pooling and exponential-history baselines for a separately measured future target. Ask whether older relations contribute after current content is accounted for, and whether the useful history differs by task and role. Do not estimate a memory mechanism from autocorrelation created by the smoothing operation itself.
- **P06 — Stress-test the clock.** Hold the text order/content fixed while changing message packaging and measuring history in messages, tokens or elapsed time where valid timestamps exist. Test whether apparent half-lives and qualitative conclusions survive sensible changes. Compare token/time-aware formulations with message-index decay. Mark unavailable or unreliable timestamps explicitly.

**Builds on:** prefix experiments, filtering definitions and calibrated observation times. **Evidence against an intrinsic semantic half-life:** the estimate mainly tracks arbitrary segmentation, fitted objective or encoder. A task-specific predictive timescale could still be useful and should be described at that scope.

### Q4. Do temporal paths reveal development that static summaries miss?

- **P07 — Detect independently defined episodes.** Compare corrected trajectory statistics with adjacent similarity, fixed windows and standard change-point baselines on separately annotated switches, returns and revisions. Include gradual transitions, repeated wording, long assistant replies and tool-generated text as counterexamples. Offline boundary detection and prospective detection require separate evaluations.
- **P08 — Compare static and temporal information.** For the same external outcome and available prefix, compare content/static-network summaries with added sequence features. Calibrate on fixed graphs and planted histories with equal content; use uniform and local-persistence-preserving order controls where their assumptions fit. Report null calibration and positive-control power before interpreting a negative real-data result.

**Builds on:** temporal calibration, episodes, the published static-network paper and accepted hierarchy paper. **Evidence against the proposed sequence contribution:** matched static/content baselines explain the result, or local persistence reproduces it. Existing eleven-archive results remain development evidence. A corrected method alone does not establish a new empirical finding.

### Q5. Which state representation earns its complexity and cost?

- **P09 — Reconstruction at matched cost.** Reuse valid prefix results as development evidence, then compare weighted pooling, last-message, rolling windows and contextual prefix embeddings on untouched public conversations using feasible local resources. Include within-conversation discrimination, error by length/task, runtime and storage. High absolute cosine is insufficient if simple pooling performs similarly.
- **P10 — Test richer dynamics.** Compare first-order filtering with lagged/inertial or separate interacting states for one prespecified held-out prediction target. Match tuning effort, regularization and effective capacity against ordinary autoregressive/state-space baselines. Include simulated cases where richer dynamics are known to matter and cases where they should not. Any attractor claim needs a definition, stability test and comparison with ordinary topic recurrence before it becomes a separate experiment.

**Builds on:** Embedding Dynamics, Semantic Dynamics and embflow. **Evidence against the richer model:** no practically meaningful gain over simpler alternatives at adequate precision, poor calibration or gains caused by extra capacity. Successful reconstruction is distinct from forecasting, and neither alone proves a physical mechanism.

### Q6. Which ideas are later incorporated, revised or applied?

- **P11 — Trace transformation into an artifact.** If the public release is suitable, use CoAuthor edit provenance to distinguish acceptance, verbatim survival, transformation and later incorporation. Test whether preceding conversational/event structure predicts a specifically chosen outcome beyond suggestion length, task and baseline acceptance measures. Compare carefully with published reuse and temporal-retention analyses.
- **P12 — Test transfer beyond the current exchange.** Pursue either independently scored application or long-gap reuse only after a source-specific access/linkage audit. KITE is an outcome lead with unresolved usable-export/license questions; new archive histories are a separate recurrence lead. These are different estimands and must not be pooled. Old selected archives and private examples supply hypothesis development only.

**Builds on:** temporal returns, hierarchy and Operational Memex. **Evidence against advancement:** no valid external outcome/linkage, inadequate eligible sample, or an already answered research question. Artifact retention is not demonstrated understanding. This family can remain deferred while the other five proceed.

## Shared infrastructure and measurements

Use a versioned observation schema for conversation, role, ordered message/event, timestamps and provenance, with corpus-specific adapters. Preserve reply/edit structure where available; do not flatten an editing session into a dialogue and silently treat the two as equivalent. Reuse valid embflow and calibration code after interface/measurement checks. Pin data, encoders, configurations, seeds and runtime. Synthetic planted histories establish calibration, not evidence about people.

Prefer a small number of well-understood public sources over a broad unaudited collection. PRISM or a fresh eligible WildChat partition remain general-dialogue leads; CoAuthor provides a distinct editing setting. Access, licensing, duplicates, participant linkage and usable sample sizes still need experiment-specific verification. The previously analyzed ThoughtTrace evaluation and selected archive sets are exposed data, even when a new question is asked. Their reuse must be labeled exploratory.

Across the portfolio, inspect sensitivity to role mixture, text length, topic/task composition, embedding model and geometric concentration, graph construction and time unit. Platform comparisons describe sampled settings and cannot isolate platform effects. Human judgments of integration/revision should be independent of network scores; model-generated labels require separate validation. Independent coding needs researcher time even without recruiting study participants. Without it, restrict claims to the available measurement.

## Staging and evidence rules

1. **Map and calibrate broadly.** Reuse existing findings, identify mathematical/construction effects, and run cheap discriminating checks across available families. Conduct one shared public annotation/coverage feasibility study where it can inform P01, P04 and P07. Shared annotations save work but do not become three independent replications. Start data-access checks for Q6 without making the rest depend on them.
2. **Explore on development data.** Give each executed experiment a card specifying its hypothesis, estimand, alternatives, smallest meaningful effect, split unit, compute/time bound, uncertainty method and stopping conditions before looking at its new results. Record all attempted variants, failed predictions and protocol changes. Fixed resources and useful information gained should determine expansion, not the number of favorable p-values.
3. **Confirm selected claims.** Promote roughly two or three promising or substantively corrective questions, rather than declaring all twelve confirmatory. Lock outcomes, models and sensitivity panels before opening a reserved evaluation set. Group by participant when reliable, otherwise by conversation/archive or conservative duplicate/linkage components as justified. Keep all related probes away from this holdout until selection is complete. A shared holdout cannot repeatedly become a fresh test for each new idea.
4. **Choose papers from the evidence.** For ISCS, a coherent combination could be integration prevalence, how it develops across roles, and whether network/dynamic measures capture it. The merged dynamics paper could compare state representations, timescales and richer models. These are possible packages, not promised results or a requirement to produce two papers. Cross-reference shared evidence and do not present it as independent discovery twice.

Exploration should emphasize effect sizes, uncertainty and counterexamples. Confirmatory comparisons need a prespecified family of claims and an appropriate multiplicity procedure; a corrected p-value on repeatedly explored data does not restore independence. Resample at the sampling/dependence unit, not individual turns as if independent. A negative conclusion requires calibrated power or sufficiently narrow uncertainty around a meaningful bound. Otherwise record inconclusive. Freeze practical bounds per outcome rather than inventing one universal threshold for all questions.

## Closest-work checks and provenance

The [existing literature map](../../future-ideas/iscs-2027/research-deep-dive/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) remains the main comparison. Four primary pages were rechecked October 17 while preparing this portfolio:

- [Measuring Inclusion in Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09269), a February 2026 preprint, already proposes interaction-level participation and idea-uptake measures. Q2 needs a more specific contribution than introducing a semantic alignment score.
- [Bridging Talk and Thought](https://aclanthology.org/2026.sigdial-1.48/), SIGDIAL 2026, already studies collaborative dialogue using a regulatory/communicative coding framework across nine datasets. Q1/Q2 should compare relational integration with that framework.
- [CoAuthor's official dataset](https://coauthor.stanford.edu/) documents timestamped suggestion and edit traces. This supports Q6's provenance lead, not a claim that uptake is unstudied.
- [Characterizing Human Semantic Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05971), whose record identifies ICLR 2026, already uses cumulative embedding trajectories and geometric/dynamical metrics. Q3–Q5 need independently useful measurements or discriminating predictions beyond trajectory construction.
