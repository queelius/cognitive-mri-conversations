**Research options, ranked for further discussion rather than full-paper authorization**

1. **When does spanning topics amount to integrating ideas? — first choice.**

   Build directly on the published bridge taxonomy and the hierarchy paper's multi-domain conversations. Compare three observable cases: relating concepts in a shared explanation/application; sequentially switching tasks; and listing multiple domains without connecting them. The new contribution would be a public cross-sectional estimate of these behaviors, their development across turns, and how faithfully existing network measurements identify them. The taxonomy itself is not new.

   Use a fixed public conversation sample with enough multi-topic exchanges, initially PRISM or a fresh WildChat partition. Have independent annotation identify relational/application evidence before computing graph scores. Keep ambiguous examples. Separate user, assistant, quoted and generated-context text. Compare whole-conversation co-occurrence with episode-level relations, direct-text baselines and established discourse codes; control length, topic count, task, concept frequency and role mixture. Group repeated participants or conservative linkage components, and reserve an independent evaluation sample. A global public-corpus graph is not a personal archive network.

   Smallest decisive step: a blinded annotation/coverage check, including low-scoring controls rather than selecting only apparent bridges. Continue only if integration is reliably distinguishable from switching, occurs often enough, and the proposed empirical comparison is not already answered by ENA/SIGDIAL work. A negative result could constrain an important bridge interpretation, but unreliability or a weak classifier alone is not a paper. This best matches the user's account of connections emerging through back-and-forth. Estimated feasibility check: 3–5 focused days plus independent coding time; a full study remains conditional.

2. **Which conversational developments precede successful independent application? — strongest outcome-oriented alternative, conditional on data.**

   Test a narrow sequence such as an explanation being challenged, revised and restated in the user's own application before independent success. KITE is a promising lead; its public export is not yet verified. CoAuthor provides an alternative outcome—what writers actually incorporate and transform—but writing incorporation is not independent problem-solving ability.

   Use pre-outcome turns only; compare role-aware recurrence/episode measures against task difficulty, initial skill, model, verbosity, turns and direct-text representations. Participant-level splits and uncertainty are essential; task overlap needs its own sensitivity analysis. More revision can indicate difficulty rather than cause success. Existing KITE qualitative explanations, CoAuthor named-entity reuse, and temporal-fingerprint retention analyses set a high novelty bar. Proceed only if the exact proposed measure/outcome adds something they do not already establish. Do not call observational success a causal learning effect or a long-term retention measure.

3. **What returns after a long gap: the same task, or an old idea used differently? — best reuse of temporal assets, higher data risk.**

   The calibrated return-age pipeline can locate recurrence; the substantive question is whether returns preserve, revise or recombine a prior relation. Compare against topic frequency, recency and local burst structure, with matched opportunities for a return. The already selected eleven archives are development material. Public archive links are proxies, not verified people. Within-session writing data cannot substitute for months-long histories. Promote only after sufficient independently held-out histories and reliable application labels are found; otherwise keep this as a longitudinal case-study direction.

4. **Does anonymization change which ideas appear central or connected? — reproducible methodological fallback.**

   Build on the data-paper draft and both network papers. Apply controlled masking and consistent pseudonymization to public conversation data; test whether bridge rankings, community assignments and cross-domain conclusions change despite apparently preserved semantic/retrieval utility. Compare entity-consistent transformations with matched token removal and encoder/threshold sensitivity. Independent identity-risk evaluation is separate from topology preservation. A paper requires a material, repeatable distortion and actionable mitigation beyond established privacy/utility tradeoffs. This is less aligned with the preference for a new behavioral finding, but avoids uncertain longitudinal linkage.

5. **At what level does abstraction remain useful? — longer-term hierarchy direction.**

   Replace a claim that fixed Ward cuts reveal natural levels with a question about held-out performance: which abstraction levels preserve rare exceptions, relation distinctions or usable retrieval paths? Compare flat retrieval, adaptive bipartite/hierarchical models and established memory methods. Keep the failed selective-forgetting construct and missing cross-topic gold evidence in view. Do not run another policy comparison before validating the target information and outcome.

6. **Semantic dynamics as economical prediction or reconstruction — longer-term technical direction.**

   The real prefix experiments and embflow can support a cost–error study or an independently labeled episode/return task. Compare with means, last-message representations, fixed exponential smoothing, standard change-point methods and order-preserving controls. The existing selected-sample prefix gain has uncertainty spanning zero; the universal turning claim and several motion summaries failed audit. A useful filter need not instantiate a new physical mechanism. Generic trajectory clustering, fitted decay parameters or another high-cosine demonstration would not clear the literature bar.

**Current judgment:** compare option 1 with the narrowly specified, data-conditional option 2 before investing in another implementation. Option 1 most directly extends the two conference contributions and can support a cross-sectional study without reconstructing long personal histories. Option 2 offers a stronger independent outcome if accessible data and novelty survive scrutiny. Keep options 3–6 as alternatives with explicit gates. No existing result yet establishes a new full ISCS paper; none of the completed pilot outcomes is being reversed by brainstorming.
