# Cross-sectional use, role attribution and organization by purpose

Search cutoff: 2026-10-15. **A paper that merely compares user/assistant weights, counts topic clusters, or contrasts usage groups would have weak novelty.** The original CMRI study already included role-weight ablations. Current public-data work additionally covers intent/domain taxonomies, activity differences, conversational steering, grounding, and user-reported purposes. The plausible opening is a falsifiable validity test of what CMRI-style network structure adds beyond text/topic/activity and whose contribution it represents.

This is a focused primary-source comparison, not a systematic review. [Search/source log](../checks/public-cross-section/search-log.json); [release/access audit](../audits/public-data.md); [completed bounded measurement check](../checks/public-cross-section/REPORT.md).

## Closest comparators and consequences

Uses linked histories, not only aggregate topic counts. | The central direct comparator. “Different users use AI differently” and “purpose changes over time” are already studied. ## The direct comparison with Adopt ≠ Adapt

Hicke and Tomlinson's WildChat analysis removes IP proxies with more than three countries, states or languages, or more than 161 conversations, leaving 2,522,330 conversations and 1,830,631 proxies before restricting the main analysis to pre-September-2024 data. They explicitly acknowledge weaker linkage than account/cookie-based Copilot data. Their completion validation is cautionary: human annotators agreed on 62% of sampled labels, κ=.25, and the construct was interpreted as task concreteness. These labels cannot be treated as objective successful task completion. [Paper, data and annotation sections](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29018v1)

The [pinned release](https://github.com/microsoft/adopt-adapt-wildchat/tree/b4e77e44b62ba2ddd0f7b53667b218e345faba09) supplies domain/intent/task outputs, allowing low-cost comparison to explicit task variables. Their work does not establish that a thresholded semantic graph measures a person's knowledge architecture. That gap is real, but proving that graph clusters differ across groups would not fill it: changes in topic mixture, message length, model, activity and sampling can all generate different graphs mechanically.

The stronger CMRI contribution would therefore be **incremental validity or a well-supported limit on validity**: after matched topic/activity/length and direct embedding controls, does graph organization predict an external held-out target? The unit must be named accurately—conversation, study participant or IP-linked proxy. Reusing the same ten selected WildChat archives from the temporal work is exploratory development, not independent confirmation.

## What counts as a new question

**Archive structure beyond topic/activity.** Compare within-archive organization to nulls preserving archive size, topic proportions, message lengths, model/time distributions and local repetition. Evaluate whether any residual structure is stable across thresholds/encoders and predicts an external target on new groups. A generic modularity difference between archives is not sufficient. Public IP-linked data can establish robustness of a proxy-level measurement but cannot establish person-level knowledge organization by itself.

**Attribution to user and assistant.** Ask when adding assistant text changes apparent purpose or creates connections absent from the user's independently reported aims. Separate descriptive representational attribution from behavioral causation. Use input ablation and matched assistant-message swaps as negative controls, with direct user-text and dialogue-text baselines. Strong performance of assistant-only text may reflect paraphrasing of the request, richer topic vocabulary, boilerplate or the fact that the target annotator saw the entire conversation; it does not show that the assistant originated the user's purpose.

**Organization by purpose.** Test purpose while controlling for topic. For example, programming conversations can seek explanation, produce code, revise output or evaluate alternatives; shared topical vocabulary need not imply shared purpose. The claim is defensible only if a measurement improves held-out purpose discrimination beyond topic and stage, and survives leakage controls. Released LLM intent labels are useful for developing the pipeline but insufficient as the only validity evidence.

These are candidate empirical questions rather than demonstrated contributions. Graph features are transformations of the same text embeddings: they add an inductive bias, not new observed information. They need comparisons against sufficiently strong direct-text models with comparable training and tuning budgets.

## Best no-recruitment validation path

ThoughtTrace contains raw participant reports and an LLM taxonomy applied to those reports. Its two ten-minute tasks per participant make it a short interaction study, not an archive sample. The released fields include `task_summary`, `task_expectation`, user-message `reasons`, assistant-message `reactions`, and categorical labels. The reports were hidden from the assistant; post-task fields and subsequent dialogue must remain outside any predictor claiming prefix-time access. [Collection and analysis methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20087v1)

**Proposed next hypothesis:** A graph built solely from an observed dialogue prefix improves discrimination of its user's reported reason from matched other-participant reasons, beyond direct role embeddings and topic/stage/length/model controls. A complementary hypothesis is that joint graphs produce more false purpose links than user-only graphs when assistant material is swapped within tightly matched strata. This targets added validity and representation contamination; it does not repeat the published observation that messages and thoughts differ.

Use participant-group development/evaluation splits, connect duplicate templates before splitting, freeze the reference pool and matching rules, and bootstrap participants. Raw reported text is the primary external target; LLM-assigned reason categories are secondary. A threshold such as ≥5 percentage points added paired retrieval accuracy with a lower confidence bound above zero is a planning decision to preregister, not an established effect. If a strong direct-text baseline erases the gain, or shuffled topology performs as well, drop the graph-specific claim. The public-data audit gives an operational version of this proposed check and its leakage controls.

The category annotation procedure itself uses different role contexts for reasons and reactions. A user/assistant comparison against those category labels could partly reproduce that design. Treat this as a target-construction bias to test, not independent proof of role attribution; exact labels and contextual inputs are recorded in the data audit.

PRISM offers another established external-data route: participant IDs, conversation trees, fine-grained feedback and stated preferences. It supports a preference/grounding validation question better than a claim about extensive personal knowledge histories. The actual conversation contributors are fewer than the advertised survey sample; rated branches must stay grouped. [Primary dataset paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16019), [pinned release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HannahRoseKirk/prism-alignment/tree/18ab5cfb37456f4ec8cbc00212ce54cf7b1239f6)

## Completed check and current decision

The corrected fixed WildChat role check uses 406 IP/template components from April 9–10, 2023, with 245 development and 161 evaluation conversations. Components are constructed across all 2,000 acquired source records before applying any eligibility filter; excluded rows can retain dependency bridges. Assistant-minus-user intent accuracy was +2.5 pp with a 95% interval [−5.0,+9.9]; the mixed-minus-user estimate was +3.7 pp [−0.6,+8.1]. Domain intervals also crossed zero, including the mixed-minus-user point estimate of +5.0 pp [−0.6,+10.7]. These observations fail the frozen joint effect-and-uncertainty criterion and cannot establish equivalence. They establish inexpensive public-data plumbing and warn against treating a role preference as an already demonstrated result. Prior results with incomplete grouping are preserved and superseded. [Protocol and complete evidence](../checks/public-cross-section/REPORT.md)

**ISCS judgment:** a conversation-level measurement/attribution study is a credible conditional option if an independent-purpose test finds a clear failure mechanism or stable incremental validity. A broad cross-person knowledge-network comparison is less ready because linkage and eligible archive counts remain unresolved. Neither the bounded result nor the literature justifies declaring a new graph method or a full paper ready now. The relevant next investment is an independent-target validation pilot, not another large role-weight grid.

## Additional access and novelty boundaries

The September 2026 four-country donated-history study has 1,252 contributors and 202,590 conversations, but explicitly excludes public raw-history release and proposes restricted academic access under agreement. It is a relevant comparator for topic/purpose and individual variation, not an immediate unrestricted source. [Primary paper, release policy](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38279v1)

The AI-fluency release includes a single 88.26 MB compressed annotation artifact; its [repository](https://github.com/bigspinai/bigspin-fluency-outcomes) is a realistic next source if task-complexity/interaction-mode controls are needed. This audit inspected its release metadata but did not acquire it or validate the annotations. The taxonomy's fluency labels and outcomes are model-derived; repeated inference calls or many transcript attributes are not independent human observations.
