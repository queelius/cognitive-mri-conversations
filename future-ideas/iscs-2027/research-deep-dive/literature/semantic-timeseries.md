# Semantic trajectories, time series, and measurement: critical literature map

Search and inspection date: 2026-10-15. This is a targeted scoping review with citation chasing, not a systematic or exhaustive review. Primary papers and released implementations carry technical claims. Online proposals are marked separately. Literature establishes competitors and plausible gaps; it does not establish that a proposed gap is publishable.

## The strongest novelty constraints

**Semantic trajectories already have substantial precedents.** [Nour et al., VECTOR, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14455) maps narratives into task-relevant conceptual spaces and relates trajectory properties to independently observed behavior. Its [full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14455) was inspected, including contextualization and statistical methods. For CMRI, this sets a higher validation standard than displaying clusters or naming them cognitive modes. Its task-specific representations and controlled narratives differ from heterogeneous AI archives. Publication status beyond the retrieved preprint was not established.

[Toro-Hernández, Vieira Filho and Cabral-Carvalho, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05971v1) uses embeddings of cumulative concept lists, finite-difference velocity/acceleration, and multiple encoders on four datasets. This directly occupies much of the proposed trajectory framing. CMRI's weighted pooling of separately embedded messages is a different approximation to prefix embedding; generic accumulation and derivatives are insufficient novelty. We inspected methods and [released code](https://github.com/jesuinovieira/semtraj-iclr2026). A bounded analytical audit finds that its released median-split entropy statistic is determined by length when distances have no ties: see [protocol and evidence](../checks/metric-calibration/PROTOCOL.md). This criticism is restricted to that statistic, not the paper's entire empirical contribution.

[Chang et al., 2026 manuscript with journal DOI](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11371v1) studies semantic timescales, autocorrelation, spectral summaries, and shuffles separating content order from timing. The author version points to [Computer Speech & Language](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csl.2026.102013); search metadata lists a January 2027 issue, so issue date must not be mistaken for first availability. Its speech-time axis differs from message index. This is direct prior for claims that semantic signals exhibit temporal dependence beyond composition. It also illustrates why overlapping windows, duration, and the choice of scalar signal deserve explicit controls.

**Interaction recurrence and hierarchy are older than transformer embeddings.** [Angus, Smith and Wiles, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASL.2012.2189566) treats communication as coupled time series with multi-participant recurrence; an author-uploaded full text was inspected. [Angus and Wiles, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5024809) extends conceptual recurrence into speaker/group semantic networks, with comparison to expert discourse coding. These weaken novelty claims for conversation-as-dynamics or combining interaction networks with semantics. A modern contribution needs better measurement, useful generalization, or an independently tested mechanism.

**Temporal sophistication need not improve prediction.** [Chowdhury, SIGDIAL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.sigdial-1.31/) compares static and temporal models of collaborative dyads. The proceedings abstract explicitly reports stronger prediction from static interaction features while describing exploratory trajectory interpretations. Only the proceedings abstract was inspected. This is a useful negative comparator: a trajectory feature can be descriptive without adding predictive value. Its acoustic/task setting is different from CMRI.

## Aggregation and dynamical baselines

- [Arora, Liang and Ma, ICLR 2017](https://openreview.net/forum?id=SyK00v5xx): weighted pooling is an established strong baseline for sentence representations. The OpenReview page was challenge-blocked; official bibliographic identifiers were corroborated, but this run did not retrieve the primary full text. Do not base a detailed derivation on secondary summaries.
- [Ma et al., MEGA, ICLR 2023](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=qNLe3iq2El): exponential moving averages already encode sequence dependence within neural architectures. The author [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10655) and primary paper excerpt were inspected. CMRI's descriptive filters have a different objective, but an EMA recurrence alone is not a new physical law.
- [Bamler and Mandt, ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/bamler17a.html): latent diffusion models of changing word embeddings are established. This is semantic change across historical time, not within-dialogue motion; cite to clarify the distinction rather than claiming identical tasks.
- [Fermanian, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2020.107148): path-signature features are a candidate order-sensitive baseline if a prediction task emerges. The primary publisher abstract/introduction and author preprint were inspected; implementation inspection is still required before use. They are not an automatic solution to semantic construct validity.

For a bounded CMRI study, mean embedding, last-message embedding, rolling mean, fixed EMA, simple lagged predictors and conditional randomization should precede sophisticated sequence models. Whether AR/state-space or signature models are appropriate depends on the selected outcome and actual sample size. Message-index half-life cannot be interpreted as elapsed-time persistence without timestamps and a time model.

## Episode detection is crowded, but supplies independent evaluation

[SuperDialseg, EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.249/) offers document-grounded dialogue segmentation and a broad baseline suite. The [official repository](https://github.com/Coldog2333/SuperDialseg) advertises MIT licensing and links the data on Drive. Repository commit `3e8de3d9c87b45904f7e2985f144cae4eef46212` was inspected through the GitHub API; the data are not included in that tree. Raw dataset access and participant independence were not fully verified here. Document-reference transitions are a narrower target than cognitive episodes.

[Gao et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02747) already learns topic-aware dialogue representations for segmentation. [Def-DTS, Findings of ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1066/) adds structured reasoning and intent classification. Their primary abstracts/paper excerpts were inspected; no reproduction was attempted. A derivative-based boundary detector needs to beat inexpensive cosine/window baselines before competing with these systems.

[Jia and Diaz-Rodriguez, Embed-KCPD, January 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18788v1) explicitly combines sentence embeddings with kernel change-point detection and dependence-aware analysis. Methods and abstract were inspected, not every proof. A new claim that text segmentation is change-point detection is already occupied. An online constraint, transport across roles/domains, or calibrated uncertainty could still be a distinct research question, subject to further search.

[Pan et al., SeCom, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/e56f394bbd4f0ec81393d767caa5a31b-Abstract-Conference.html) already uses topical segmentation plus compression for conversational memory and evaluates segmentation separately. The [official implementation](https://github.com/microsoft/SeCom) was inspected at README level. Combining CMRI episodes with retrieval therefore needs incremental evidence beyond changed granularity.

## Ideas posted online: inspiration, not validation

- [TraceScope](https://github.com/Pixedar/TraceScope), public repository inspected 2026-10-15: visualizes embedding trajectories and fits flow models, including agent traces. Its own documentation cautions that examples need statistical validation. It shows that a flow-field visualization is not a distinctive contribution by itself. Projection artifacts and external task outcomes are possible research questions.
- [Baladi, DSFT archival record](https://zenodo.org/records/20303214), posted 2026-05-20: proposes semantic force components and synthetic dynamical regimes. The Zenodo record labels its resource a journal article, but the inspected record does not establish an independently reviewed venue. Treat as an informal archived framework; no empirical conclusions imported.
- [Audria, topic-drift technical post](https://audria.tech/blogs/topic-drift-detection/), March 2026: proposes streaming semantic change-point detection. Read as a source of engineering questions; the paper comparisons above carry the technical evidence.
- [Nour's author announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/matthew-nour_llms-llm-psychiatry-activity-7374714916974370816-uufQ) led back to the VECTOR primary manuscript. Clinical aspirations in an announcement are not evidence for clinical use of CMRI.

## What remains plausible

The clearest opening is a **calibrated measurement study**: identify which semantic-motion statistics actually contain order information after controlling geometry, endpoints, role schedule, filtering and length; then test whether that surviving information corresponds to an independently defined behavior or outcome. A useful negative result would characterize a commonly used failure mode across methods and datasets, not merely report that one proposed CMRI feature fails.

A longer-term alternative is a validated approximation to prefix representations with an explicit error/cost/usefulness tradeoff. That needs held-out selection, difficult within-conversation prefix negatives, order perturbations, truncation controls, and downstream evidence. The present prefix cosine result cannot establish an identifiable semantic force model.
