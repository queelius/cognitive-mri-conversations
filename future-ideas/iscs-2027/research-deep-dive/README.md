## Cross-sectional and longitudinal work together

A cross-sectional design is worth pursuing **for generalization and attribution**, not merely for another table of network differences. Task, topic composition, activity, assistant vocabulary, graph coverage and representation choices can all change topology. A graph-specific result must add predictive or explanatory value beyond these controls and sufficiently strong direct-text baselines.

The available public sources support different claims. ThoughtTrace offers participant-linked short tasks and independently reported reasons/reactions, making it a plausible external criterion. PRISM offers participant ratings and preferences. Neither is a rich lifetime archive. WildChat supplies large naturalistic conversation collections, but hashed IPs remain linkage proxies, and the inspected shard contained few long histories. Raw self-reports and model-assigned labels are distinct; ThoughtTrace's category-annotation prompts themselves use different role contexts. [Data/access/licensing audit](audits/public-data.md)

The recommended pilot therefore uses **independent participants for evaluation** and **within-conversation order/role controls for calibration**. It can test whether a trajectory contains useful sequence information without claiming a platform caused a change in cognition. It must exclude retrospective fields, later turns and target reports from representation construction, and join duplicate/template components before filtering or splitting.

## Ranked next investments

1. **Public semantic-measurement calibration and external validation.** Best new question; 1–2 focused weeks to decide feasibility and scientific value. A compact 8-page paper is plausible only if it finds a consequential generalizable failure or a stable incremental result. Avoid a broad semantic-physics manuscript.
2. **Corrected temporal networks.** Best fallback and most mature writing base; approximately one measurement/calibration week followed by 4–6 focused weeks for a defensible revision. Existing randomization methods and manuscript overlap constrain novelty.
3. **Cross-sectional graph attribution.** Integrate one prespecified representation comparison into the first pilot. Defer rich-personal-archive comparisons until linkage and adequate eligible histories are established. A generic role-weight sweep is not enough.
4. **Prefix approximation.** Retain as a longer-term computational question about error, cost and downstream usefulness. The current selected sample and selection-sensitive uncertainty do not warrant a method paper yet.
5. **Projection-aware concept organization.** Strong corrective work; a separate contribution requires public multi-corpus residual structure or prediction beyond known projection effects.

## What would change this decision

Promote the new measurement paper if an independent public target is usable and either (a) a prespecified representation gives a practically meaningful, uncertainty-supported improvement beyond strong controls, or (b) calibrated positive controls and sufficiently precise public evidence establish an important limit that corrects a substantive existing inference. A negative paper needs more than failure to reject or a collection of bugs.

Return to temporal first if ThoughtTrace/PRISM cannot support the intended independent target, eligible sequences are too short, or the contribution reduces to known filtering identities without material empirical consequence. Promote prefix approximation only after public held-out error/cost/usefulness evidence; promote memory only after a necessary-cross-topic-support construct and structural proxy are independently validated. A distinctive physics theory would need identifiable assumptions and held-out predictions that ordinary filtering/sequence models fail—not additional terminology for the same recurrence.

Remaining uncertainties include event-order provenance in the private embedding DB, corpus release/count lineage, extraction provenance, validity of participant reports for the intended task, public sample coverage, and completeness of novelty search. They have specific resolution steps in the briefs; none is treated as a positive finding.
