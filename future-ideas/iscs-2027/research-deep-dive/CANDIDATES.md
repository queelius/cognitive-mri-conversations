# Competing directions and disposition

2026-10-15. Rankings are research judgments, not numerical acceptance probabilities. “Pursue” here means a bounded next validation step; **no candidate is certified ready for a full paper**. Closest-work comparisons are in the three [literature maps](literature/SEARCH-LOG.md) and [bibliography](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md).

| A | What does semantic motion measure? Calibration plus public external validation | **Pursue first**, conditional ISCS short/long paper | Several exact failure modes now have evidence, including one external metric; useful filter/order effects also survive. Needs a generalizable result beyond a bug list. Closest trajectory/recurrence literature demands independent validation. |

## Comparative judgment for the five strongest

| Candidate | Scientific value if successful | Novelty position | Feasibility with current assets | Societal relevance | ISCS fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: measurement validity | High: separates interpretable sequence evidence from construction | Conditional: calibration is established; systematic public failure/validity result would be the advance | High for 1–2 week gate; moderate for full evidence | Limits misleading profiles and cognitive inferences | Strong if tied to dynamics/nulls; short paper plausible |
| B: cross-sectional attribution | High: whose purpose does a conversation graph represent? | Crowded usage literature; external incremental validity is the gap | Moderate for participant-level short tasks; low for verified rich archives | Strong for profiling and personalization | Strong computational social science fit |
| C: temporal calibration | High corrective value; narrower theoretical advance | Familiar nulls; specific observation model and independent empirical calibration needed | Highest manuscript maturity; measurement repairs required | Prevents overreading apparent learning/growth | Strong complex-network fit |
| D: prefix approximation | Clear computational question, weaker cognitive claim | Strong pooling/sequence competitors | Good cached starting point, limited validation cohort | Indirect efficiency/reproducibility benefit | Moderate unless behavior or multiscale structure matters |
| E: projection residuals | Strong correction; potentially useful corpus diagnostic | Extensive bipartite/topic-model prior | Moderate; extraction provenance and public replication cost | Limits spurious memory claims | Strong network fit, weaker standalone contribution today |

Why A edges C for **new research**: the audit now identifies a cross-cutting measurement question, live primary competitors and public external targets. Why C remains the fallback: its manuscript and archive analyses are much more mature. This revises the October 14 default, which was based on a narrower comparison after the forgetting pilot. If A's public target is unusable, or its novelty reduces to already-known algebra with no material empirical consequence, C should immediately regain priority.

Cross-sectional versus longitudinal is not a binary choice. Use independent participants/archives for generalization and within-conversation order interventions for measurement calibration. Neither alone identifies causal effects of platforms on cognition. The five [detailed briefs](BRIEFS.md) keep these distinctions explicit.
